<a id="socrates"></a>
### 🤔 Socrates (470–399 BC)
**School:** Ancient Greek Philosophy · Ethics

Socrates was the gadfly of Athens. He wrote nothing himself — everything we know about him comes from his students Plato and Xenophon. His method, the *elenchus*, consisted of asking seemingly innocent questions that ultimately shook the deepest assumptions of his interlocutors. He believed that true wisdom begins with the realization of one's own ignorance: "I know that I know nothing." He was sentenced to death for "corrupting the youth" and "not honoring the gods of the city," but refused to flee and drank the hemlock. His legacy is the conviction that an unexamined life is not worth living.

<a id="nietzsche"></a>
###  Friedrich Nietzsche (1844–1900)
**School:** Existentialism · Philosophy of Life

Nietzsche was the philosopher with the hammer. He mercilessly attacked traditional morality, Christianity, and the "herd mentality" of his time. In *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*, he introduced the *Übermensch* (Overman) — the human who creates their own values beyond good and evil. His most famous statement, "God is dead," was not a triumphant cry but a diagnosis: the collapse of absolute truths leaves humanity in an existential vacuum. He wrote in aphorisms, full of metaphors about lightning, eagles, and mountaintops. Nietzsche wanted no followers; he wanted us to find the courage to affirm our own lives, despite suffering. He ended his life in mental collapse, but his ideas shaped the twentieth century.

<a id="little-red-riding-hood"></a>
### 🐺 Little Red Riding Hood
**School:** Fairy Tale Wisdom · Folk Tradition

Little Red Riding Hood is not a philosopher in the traditional sense, but her story contains one of humanity's oldest and deepest lessons: appearances deceive. The fairy tale, recorded by Perrault and the Brothers Grimm, tells of a girl walking through the dark forest who encounters a wolf disguised as her grandmother. "But grandmother, why do you have such big eyes?" — that question is the primal form of all critical philosophy: why are things not what they seem? Little Red Riding Hood sees with childlike innocence what adults forget: that those in power, institutions, and stories often wear a mask. Her wisdom is the wisdom of the naive question that cuts unexpectedly deep.

<a id="plato"></a>
### 🏛️ Plato (428–348 BC)
**School:** Ancient Greek Philosophy · Idealism

Plato was the student of Socrates and the teacher of Aristotle. He founded the Academy in Athens, the first university of the Western world. His core idea is the Theory of Forms: behind the changing, sensory world lies an eternal, unchanging reality of perfect Ideas. In his famous *Allegory of the Cave*, he describes people chained up seeing only shadows — the philosopher is the one who breaks free and beholds the true light. He wrote dialogues featuring Socrates, addressing justice, love, beauty, and the ideal state. His influence on Western philosophy is so great that Whitehead said: "The whole of Western philosophy is a series of footnotes to Plato."

<a id="aristotle"></a>
###  Aristotle (384–322 BC)
**School:** Ancient Greek Philosophy · Empiricism · Logic

Aristotle was Plato's student, but went in a completely different direction. Where Plato looked to the heavens, Aristotle looked to the earth. He believed that knowledge begins with observation and classification. He wrote about almost everything: biology, physics, ethics, politics, logic, poetics, and metaphysics. He developed formal logic (the syllogism) and the concept of the *Golden Mean* in ethics: virtue always lies between two extremes, such as courage between cowardice and recklessness. He founded the Lyceum in Athens and was the teacher of Alexander the Great. His systematic approach laid the foundation for the scientific method.

<a id="marx"></a>
### ✊ Karl Marx (1818–1883)
**School:** Marxism · Historical Materialism

Marx was a German philosopher, economist, and revolutionary who changed the course of history. In *Das Kapital* and the *Communist Manifesto* (with Engels), he analyzed capitalism as a system based on the exploitation of the working class by the owning class. His core idea is that history is driven by class struggle — the conflict between the *haves* and the *have-nots*. He introduced concepts like *surplus value* (the profit the capitalist extracts from the worker's labor) and *ideology* (the ideas the ruling class uses to legitimize its power). His work inspired revolutions worldwide and remains one of the most influential and controversial frameworks in history.

<a id="hannah-arendt"></a>
### 🔍 Hannah Arendt (1906–1975)
**School:** Political Philosophy · Existentialism

Hannah Arendt was a German-American thinker who fled Nazism and provided one of the sharpest analyses of power, totalitarianism, and evil. In *Eichmann in Jerusalem*, she introduced the concept of the "banality of evil": terrible crimes are not always committed by monsters, but by ordinary people who obediently follow orders without thinking. In *The Human Condition*, she distinguished three forms of human activity: *labor* (survival), *work* (making things), and *action* (conducting politics in the public realm). She warned that totalitarianism arises when people withdraw from public life and cease to think.

<a id="spinoza"></a>
### 💎 Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677)
**School:** Rationalism · Pantheism

Spinoza was a Dutch-Jewish philosopher who was excommunicated from the synagogue for his radical ideas. In his masterpiece *Ethics*, written in the style of a mathematical proof with definitions, axioms, and propositions, he argued that God and Nature are one and the same substance. There is no personal God who judges or punishes; there is only the one, infinite substance of which everything is an expression. Humans are not "a kingdom within a kingdom" but subject to the same necessary laws as the rest of nature. Freedom does not consist in escaping these laws, but in understanding them. His work was considered heresy for centuries, but later influenced Einstein, Goethe, and the Enlightenment.

<a id="camus"></a>
### 🪨 Albert Camus (1913–1960)
**School:** Absurdism · Existentialism

Camus was a French-Algerian writer and philosopher who won the Nobel Prize for Literature. His core idea is the absurd: humans long for meaning in a universe that offers none. In *The Myth of Sisyphus*, he describes humanity as Sisyphus, eternally pushing a rock uphill only for it to roll back down. But Camus is no nihilist: he chooses *revolt*. "One must imagine Sisyphus happy." In *The Plague*, he shows how ordinary people unite against evil, not out of heroism, but out of simple human solidarity. He died at age 46 in a car accident, with an unfinished manuscript in his bag.

<a id="confucius"></a>
###  Confucius (551–479 BC)
**School:** Confucianism · Chinese Philosophy

Confucius (Kong Qiu) was a Chinese teacher, politician, and philosopher whose ideas profoundly shaped East Asian civilization. He believed that social harmony arises through *ren* (humaneness), *li* (ritual and etiquette), and respect for the five fundamental relationships: ruler-subject, father-son, husband-wife, elder brother-younger brother, and friend-friend. The *junzi* (noble person) strives for virtue and self-cultivation, not personal gain. His sayings are collected in the *Analects*. Confucius was no mystic; he was a practical thinker who believed that good governance begins with the good example of the leader.

<a id="lao-tze"></a>
### ☯️ Lao Tze (6th century BC)
**School:** Taoism · Chinese Philosophy

Lao Tze is the legendary author of the *Tao Te Ching*, one of the most translated books in the world. He is considered the founder of Taoism. His core idea is the *Tao* (the Way): the ineffable, natural order underlying everything. He preached *wu wei* (non-action or effortless action): the wise person does not force, but moves with the natural flow. Softness overcomes hardness, water wears away stone, and the wise leader rules by not ruling. His style is poetic, paradoxical, and minimalist. Whether Lao Tze actually existed is uncertain, but his influence on Chinese culture, art, medicine, and spirituality is immense.

<a id="marcus-aurelius"></a>
### 🏔️ Marcus Aurelius (121–180 AD)
**School:** Stoicism

Marcus Aurelius was Emperor of Rome and a philosopher. His *Meditations* — a personal diary never intended for publication — is one of the most read philosophical works of all time. As a Stoic, he believed in the dichotomy of control: some things are within our power (our thoughts, reactions, choices), others are not (the weather, others' actions, death). Wisdom consists in accepting what you cannot change and cultivating virtue in what you can. He wrote at the front, during wars and plagues, and his words testify to a deep inner calm amidst chaos. "You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength."

<a id="buddha"></a>
###  Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha (c. 563–483 BC)
**School:** Buddhism

Siddhartha Gautama was a prince who left his palace after seeing old age, sickness, and death. After years of asceticism and meditation, he attained enlightenment under a bodhi tree and became the Buddha ("the Awakened One"). His core teaching is the Four Noble Truths: (1) life is *dukkha* (suffering/unsatisfactoriness), (2) the cause is *tanha* (attachment/craving), (3) there is an end to suffering, and (4) the path to it is the Noble Eightfold Path (right understanding, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration). He taught that everything is impermanent (*anicca*) and that the self is an illusion (*anatta*). His message was one of compassion, nonviolence, and inner liberation.

<a id="leibniz"></a>
### ️ Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)
**School:** Rationalism · Optimism

Leibniz was a German polymath who independently invented calculus alongside Newton. In his philosophy, he argued that God, being infinitely good and omnipotent, created the *best of all possible worlds*. Every evil and suffering fits into a greater, invisible harmony. His *Monadology* describes reality as composed of countless simple substances (monads) synchronized by a "pre-established harmony." Voltaire ridiculed him in *Candide*, but Leibniz's optimism was not naivety — it was a deep rational trust in the order of the universe. He also dreamed of a universal language of human thought.

<a id="schopenhauer"></a>
### 😔 Arthur Schopenhauer (1788–1860)
**School:** Pessimism · Will-Philosophy

Schopenhauer was the great pessimist of Western philosophy. In his masterwork *The World as Will and Representation*, he argued that reality is driven by a blind, irrational *Will* — an insatiable drive manifesting in everything, from gravity to human desire. Life is a pendulum swinging between pain (when we desire something) and boredom (when we have it). The only solace lies in aesthetic contemplation (especially music, which he considered the highest art form) and compassion for the suffering of all beings. He was deeply influenced by the Upanishads and Buddhism. His work influenced Nietzsche, Freud, Wagner, and Wittgenstein.

*[⬆ Back to top](#top)*

<a id="descartes"></a>
###  René Descartes (1596–1650)
**School:** Rationalism · Dualism

Descartes is called the "father of modern philosophy." In his *Meditations on First Philosophy*, he applied methodical doubt: he doubted everything that was doubtful — the senses, mathematics, even the existence of the external world — until he found something that could not be doubted: the fact that he was doubting. *Cogito, ergo sum* ("I think, therefore I am") became the foundation of modern philosophy. He distinguished two substances: *res cogitans* (thinking mind) and *res extensa* (extended matter), a dualism that would dominate Western thought for centuries. He was also a brilliant mathematician who developed analytic geometry.

<a id="edward-de-bono"></a>
### 💡 Edward de Bono (1933–2021)
**School:** Creative Thinking · Cognitive Psychology

Edward de Bono was a Maltese physician and psychologist who coined the term *lateral thinking*. He argued that traditional, logical ("vertical") thinking is insufficient for solving complex problems; you must also think "sideways," breaking assumptions and making unexpected connections. His best-known method is the *Six Thinking Hats*: each hat represents a thinking mode (white=facts, red=emotions, black=criticism, yellow=optimism, green=creativity, blue=process). He wrote more than 80 books and advised governments and companies worldwide. His work made creative thinking a trainable skill rather than a mysterious talent.

<a id="avicenna"></a>
### ⚕️ Avicenna / Ibn Sina (980–1037)
**School:** Islamic Philosophy · Aristotelianism

Avicenna was a Persian polymath and one of the greatest thinkers of the Islamic Golden Age. His *Canon of Medicine* was the standard medical textbook for centuries in both the Islamic world and Europe. In philosophy, he attempted to reconcile Aristotle with Islamic theology. His famous *Floating Man* thought experiment asks you to imagine floating in the air without sensory input — you would still know you exist, proving the soul is independent of the body. He developed an elaborate metaphysics of necessity and contingency that deeply influenced Thomas Aquinas and Western scholasticism.

<a id="gandhi"></a>
### 🕊️ Mahatma Gandhi (1869–1948)
**School:** Nonviolent Resistance · Hindu Philosophy

Gandhi was an Indian lawyer and political leader who led India's independence through *satyagraha* (truth-force) and *ahimsa* (nonviolence). He believed that means determine ends: you cannot build a just society with unjust methods. His philosophy was rooted in the *Bhagavad Gita*, Christianity (especially the Sermon on the Mount), and the work of Tolstoy and Thoreau. He lived simply, wore hand-spun clothing, and used hunger strikes as a political weapon. His influence extends to Martin Luther King, Nelson Mandela, and all nonviolent movements that came after him. "Be the change you want to see in the world."

<a id="noam-chomsky"></a>
###  Noam Chomsky (1928–present)
**School:** Anarcho-syndicalism · Media Critique · Linguistics

Chomsky is an American linguist, philosopher, and political activist. In linguistics, he revolutionized the field with his theory of universal grammar. In political philosophy, he is known for his media critique. In *Manufacturing Consent* (with Edward Herman), he developed the propaganda model: media in democratic societies do not function as watchdogs but as filters serving the interests of the powerful, through five mechanisms (ownership, advertisers, sourcing, flak, and anti-ideology). He has analyzed U.S. foreign policy and the role of media in manufacturing public consent for decades. He is considered one of the most cited living intellectuals.

<a id="kierkegaard"></a>
### 😰 Søren Kierkegaard (1813–1855)
**School:** Existentialism · Christian Philosophy

Kierkegaard was a Danish philosopher considered the father of existentialism. He opposed Hegel's abstract system and argued that truth is subjective: what matters is not what you know, but how you live. He described three stages of existence: the *aesthetic* (pleasure), the *ethical* (duty), and the *religious* (faith). Transitioning between these stages requires a "leap" — a decision that cannot be rationally justified. Anxiety (*Angst*) is for him the "dizziness of freedom": the realization that we are radically free to choose, and that freedom is terrifying. His work influenced Heidegger, Sartre, Camus, and the entire existentialist tradition.

<a id="thomas-aquinas"></a>
### ✝️ Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)
**School:** Scholasticism · Thomism

Thomas Aquinas was an Italian Dominican friar and the greatest philosopher-theologian of the Middle Ages. In his monumental *Summa Theologiae*, he united Christian faith with Aristotelian philosophy. He developed the *Five Ways* — five rational proofs for God's existence, based on motion, causality, contingency, gradation, and purpose. He believed that faith and reason do not contradict but complement each other: reason can bring us to the threshold of the divine, but faith takes over from there. His natural law theory influenced Western legal thought to this day.

<a id="william-of-ockham"></a>
### 🔪 William of Ockham (c. 1287–1347)
**School:** Scholasticism · Nominalism

William of Ockham was an English Franciscan friar and philosopher. He is best known for *Ockham's Razor*: the principle that one should not multiply entities beyond necessity. In other words: if there are multiple explanations for a phenomenon, choose the simplest. This principle became a cornerstone of the scientific method. In philosophy, he was a nominalist: he believed that universal concepts (like "humanity" or "redness") do not actually exist, but are merely names we give to groups of individual things. His radical ideas about the separation of church and state brought him into conflict with the Pope, and he fled to the court of the German Emperor.

<a id="giordano-bruno"></a>
### 🔥 Giordano Bruno (1548–1600)
**School:** Renaissance Natural Philosophy · Hermeticism

Bruno was an Italian Dominican friar, mathematician, and freethinker who was burned at the stake by the Inquisition. He defended Copernican heliocentrism and went further: he proposed that the universe is infinite, with countless worlds and suns. This was revolutionary in a time when Earth was still considered the center of the universe. He combined this with hermetic and magical ideas: the cosmos itself is alive and permeated with divine spirit. His dialogue *De l'infinito, universo e mondi* is one of the first defenses of the infinite universe. He was considered a heretic and died as a martyr for free thought. His courage inspired later freethinkers and scientists.

<a id="hermes-trismegistus"></a>
### 🐍 Hermes Trismegistus
**School:** Hermeticism · Mystical Philosophy

Hermes Trismegistus ("thrice-greatest") is a legendary figure, a syncretic combination of the Greek god Hermes and the Egyptian god Thoth. He is considered the author of the *Hermetica*, a collection of writings from the early centuries AD that formed the basis of the Western esoteric tradition. The core of his teaching is summarized in the famous saying: "As above, so below" — the microcosm (humanity) reflects the macrocosm (the universe). The Hermetic tradition influenced Renaissance thinkers like Ficino and Pico della Mirandola, and through them, the development of modern science. His teaching of transmutation and cosmic unity remains a source of inspiration for mystics, alchemists, and philosophers.

<a id="valentinus"></a>
### 🔮 Valentinus (c. 100–160)
**School:** Gnosticism · Christian Mysticism

Valentinus was an early Christian mystic and teacher from Alexandria, and one of the most important representatives of Gnosticism. He taught that the material world was not created by the true God, but by a lower, ignorant deity (the demiurge). The true reality is the *Pleroma* — the divine realm of fullness and light. Humanity carries a divine spark trapped in the material world; salvation comes through *gnosis*, direct spiritual knowledge. His complex cosmology describes the emanation of divine aeons and the fall of Sophia (Wisdom). Although condemned as a heretic by the orthodox church, he was one of the most influential Christian thinkers of his time.

<a id="troelstra"></a>
### 🚩 Pieter Jelles Troelstra (1860–1930)
**School:** Socialism · Labor Movement

Troelstra was a Frisian lawyer, poet, and politician, and the founder of social democracy in the Netherlands. He founded the Social Democratic Workers' Party (SDAP) and was its first chairman. His famous saying "De tijd zal komen dat allen leven mogen" ("The time will come when all may live") became the anthem of the labor movement. He fought for universal suffrage, social legislation, and the emancipation of the working class. His philosophy combined Marxist class analysis with a Christian-ethical sense of justice. He was also a gifted poet; his *Fryske Sangen* are still appreciated. Troelstra's legacy is the Dutch welfare state, which he helped shape.

<a id="maria-montessori"></a>
### 📖 Maria Montessori (1870–1952)
**School:** Pedagogy · Developmental Psychology

Montessori was an Italian physician, educator, and the first female physician of Italy. She developed a revolutionary educational model based on the observation that children are naturally curious and learn best in a prepared environment where they can discover independently. Her core concepts are the *absorbent mind* (young children's ability to effortlessly absorb language and culture), *sensitive periods* (windows when children are receptive to specific learning processes), and the teacher's role as "guide" rather than authority. Her method, applied in thousands of schools worldwide, emphasizes respect for the child, practical skills, and self-discipline. Her work influenced Google founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin, among others.

<a id="einstein"></a>
### ⚛️ Albert Einstein (1879–1955)
**School:** Philosophy of Science · Pacifism

Einstein was a German-Swiss physicist who fundamentally changed physics with his theories of relativity. His special theory of relativity (1905) showed that time and space are not absolute; his general theory of relativity (1915) redefined gravity as the curvature of spacetime. He won the Nobel Prize for explaining the photoelectric effect, which helped establish quantum mechanics. But Einstein was also a profound thinker on philosophical questions: he was a determinist who did not believe in chance ("God does not play dice"), a pacifist who campaigned for nuclear disarmament, and a humanist who warned against nationalism. "Imagination is more important than knowledge."

<a id="pinocchio"></a>
### 🤥 Pinocchio
**School:** Literary Wisdom · Moral Fable

Pinocchio is the wooden puppet from Carlo Collodi's *The Adventures of Pinocchio* (1883). He is not a traditional philosopher, but his story contains one of the most universal moral lessons: the confrontation with truth. His nose grows when he lies — a brilliant metaphor for the idea that lies betray themselves. Pinocchio's journey is an allegory of moral development: from disobedience and impulsiveness to responsibility and honesty. His conscience, the Talking Cricket, represents the inner voice that warns us. Ultimately, he becomes a real boy — not through magic, but through learning self-discipline, honesty, and love for his father Geppetto. It is a story about the painful but necessary path to humanity.

<a id="snow-white"></a>
### 🍎 Snow White
**School:** Fairy Tale Wisdom · Psychological Symbolism

Snow White is the heroine of the famous fairy tale, recorded by the Brothers Grimm. Her story is a deep exploration of vanity, identity, and the search for truth. The evil queen, obsessed with her mirror ("Mirror, mirror on the wall"), represents the destructive power of narcissism and the fear of mortality. Snow White herself, with her innocent beauty, represents authenticity — she is beautiful without knowing it. The poisoned apple is a warning: not all that glitters is true. The seven dwarfs represent simple, honest labor. Ultimately, Snow White is awakened by the prince's kiss — a symbol of the victory of love and truth over deception and death.

<a id="sleeping-beauty"></a>
###  Sleeping Beauty
**School:** Fairy Tale Wisdom · Mythological Symbolism

Sleeping Beauty is the princess who sleeps for a hundred years, surrounded by a wall of thorns. Her story is one of the richest mythological allegories about time, fate, and awakening. The enchantment by the evil fairy is a symbol of unprocessed trauma or unfulfilled potential. The hundred-year sleep represents the time needed for ripening and transformation. The thorns protect her, but also isolate her — a metaphor for how we sometimes shield ourselves from the world. The prince's kiss that awakens her is not passive rescue, but the moment of awareness: the soul awakening from the sleep of unconsciousness. The story teaches us patience: some truths must ripen before they can reveal themselves.

<a id="blaise-pascal"></a>
### 🎲 Blaise Pascal (1623–1662)
**School:** Rationalism · Christian Apologetics

Pascal was a French mathematician, physicist, and philosopher, a child prodigy who wrote a treatise on conic sections at sixteen. He developed the first mechanical calculator and laid the foundations of probability theory. His *Pensées* (Thoughts), unfinished at his death, contain his famous *Pascal's Wager*: a rational argument that it is worthwhile to believe in God, because the potential gain is infinite and the loss negligible. He wrote: "Man is but a reed, the weakest in nature, but he is a *thinking* reed." He distinguished the *spirit of finesse* (intuitive insight) from the *spirit of geometry* (logical reasoning), and believed that the heart has its reasons which reason knows not.

<a id="desiderius-erasmus"></a>
### 📜 Desiderius Erasmus (1466–1536)
**School:** Renaissance Humanism

Erasmus was a Dutch humanist, theologian, and classicist, and the most influential intellectual of the Northern Renaissance. His masterpiece *In Praise of Folly* (*Moriae Encomium*) is a satirical encomium of Folly, in which he mercilessly mocks the hypocrisy of the church, the arrogance of scholars, and the follies of humanity. He advocated for tolerance, peace, and a return to the sources of Christianity (the Bible in the original languages). His Greek edition of the New Testament laid the foundation for later translations, including Luther's. He remained Catholic but refused to speak out against the Reformation, which made him suspect to both Protestants and Catholics. His ideal was the *doctus pius* — the learned pious person.

<a id="francis-bacon"></a>
### 🔬 Francis Bacon (1561–1626)
**School:** Empiricism · Philosophy of Science

Bacon was an English philosopher, statesman, and essayist, considered the father of modern empiricism. In *Novum Organum* (1620), he advocated a new method of scientific inquiry: instead of reasoning from abstract principles (deduction), we must begin with careful observation and experiment (induction). He identified the *Idols of the Mind* — systematic thinking errors that distort our perception: the Idols of the Tribe (human tendencies), the Cave (individual prejudices), the Marketplace (misleading language), and the Theater (dogmatic philosophies). His famous saying "Knowledge is power" (*ipsa scientia potestas est*) became the motto of modern science. He died of pneumonia contracted during an experiment with snow.

<a id="marsilio-ficino"></a>
### 🌟 Marsilio Ficino (1433–1499)
**School:** Renaissance Neoplatonism · Florentine Academy

Ficino was an Italian priest, philosopher, and translator, and the central figure of the Florentine Academy under the patronage of Cosimo de' Medici. He translated the complete works of Plato into Latin, giving the Western world access to the full Platonic tradition for the first time. His own philosophy, *Neoplatonism*, combined Plato with Christian theology and Hermetic ideas. His core concept is *cosmica amor* (cosmic love): a universal force that connects everything and draws it toward the divine. In *De amore* (On Love), he describes how love elevates the soul from earthly beauty to divine beauty. He believed in the *harmonia mundi* — the harmony of the spheres — and in humanity's central position in the universe.

### ⛪ Martin Luther (1483–1546)
**School:** Reformation · Protestantism

Luther was a German Augustinian monk, theologian, and professor who sparked the Reformation. On October 31, 1517, he nailed his *95 Theses* to the door of the Castle Church in Wittenberg, criticizing the sale of indulgences and other abuses in the Catholic Church. His core ideas were *sola scriptura* (Scripture alone is authoritative), *sola fide* (justification by faith alone), and *sola gratia* (by grace alone). He translated the Bible into German, which standardized the German language and promoted literacy. His defiance of Pope and Emperor ("Here I stand, I can do no other") became a symbol of freedom of conscience. His work led to Protestantism, but also to the Peasants' War and religious wars that he later regretted.

<a id="jean-jacques-rousseau"></a>
### 🌳 Jean-Jacques Rousseau (1712–1778)
**School:** Romanticism · Enlightenment Philosophy

Rousseau was a Genevan philosopher, writer, and composer whose work inspired the French Revolution and Romanticism. In *Discourse on Inequality*, he argued that humans are naturally good but corrupted by society and private property. His famous opening line of *The Social Contract* — "Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains" — became a revolutionary cry. He introduced the concept of the *volonté générale* (general will): the collective will of the people pursuing the common good. In *Émile*, he developed a revolutionary educational approach centered on the child. He was also a pioneer of Romanticism with his *Reveries of the Solitary Walker*. His ideas influenced modern democracy and education.

### ✉️ Belle van Zuylen / Isabelle de Charrière (1740–1805)
**School:** Enlightenment · Epistolary Literature

Belle van Zuylen was a Dutch writer who spent most of her life in Switzerland. She wrote letters, novels, operas, and essays in French, and corresponded with great minds like Benjamin Constant and James Boswell. Her best-known work, *Lettres neuchâteloises* (Letters from Neuchâtel), is a masterpiece of epistolary literature in which she sharply observes the social conventions, hypocrisy, and limitations of her time. She advocated for women's emancipation, intellectual independence, and women's right to self-development. Her style is elegant, ironic, and psychologically subtle. She is considered one of the most important Enlightenment thinkers, but was underestimated for centuries because she wrote in French and was not taken seriously as a woman.

### 👑 Queen Christina of Sweden (1626–1689)
**School:** Renaissance Humanism · Freethought

Christina was Queen of Sweden who abdicated the throne at age 33 — one of the most remarkable abdications in history. She was exceptionally learned, spoke multiple languages, and invited the greatest thinkers of her time to her court, including Descartes (who died in Sweden). She was fascinated by philosophy, mathematics, and art, and assembled one of Europe's largest libraries. Her refusal to marry and her intellectual ambitions made her controversial. After her abdication, she converted to Catholicism and moved to Rome, where she became a center for intellectuals and artists. She is seen as a symbol of freedom of mind over political power.

<a id="anna-tumarkin"></a>
### ⚖️ Anna Tumarkin (1875–1951)
**School:** Academic Philosophy · Ethics

Tumarkin was a Russian-Swiss philosopher and the first female professor of philosophy in Europe. She studied under the famous philosopher Karl Joel in Basel and wrote her dissertation on the Stoics. In 1924, she was appointed full professor in Basel — an unprecedented achievement at a time when women barely had access to academia. Her work focused on ethics, the history of philosophy, and the relationship between thinking and acting. She emphasized that true knowledge requires moral responsibility: intellectual integrity is not a luxury but a duty. She advocated throughout her life for the emancipation of women in science and became a role model for generations of female academics.

<a id="mary-wollstonecraft"></a>
###  Mary Wollstonecraft (1759–1797)
**School:** Feminism · Enlightenment Philosophy

Wollstonecraft was an English writer, philosopher, and pioneer of feminism. In her groundbreaking *A Vindication of the Rights of Woman* (1792), she argued that women are not inferior to men, but have merely received insufficient education. She attacked Rousseau's ideas that women should only be educated for domestic life and demanded equal access to rational development. She believed that reason is the common foundation of all humans, regardless of gender. Her life was as unconventional as her thought: she had a daughter out of wedlock, married the philosopher William Godwin, and died of puerperal fever after giving birth to her daughter Mary (who would later write *Frankenstein*). Her work laid the foundation for modern feminism.

<a id="hadewijch"></a>
### 🕊️ Hadewijch (13th century)
**School:** Middle Dutch Mysticism · Brabant Mysticism

Hadewijch was a Brabant mystic from the thirteenth century, one of the most important medieval female thinkers. She wrote in Middle Dutch and is known for her *Strophic Poems*, *Letters on Love*, and *Visions*. Her central concept is *Minne* — divine love that is both the source and goal of the mystical life. Minne is not a gentle emotion, but a consuming, transforming force that simultaneously pains and fulfills the soul. Hadewijch describes union with God as a process of suffering, longing, and surrender. Her language is poetic, passionate, and full of paradoxes. She is counted among the Brabant mystics, along with John of Ruusbroec. Her work is a high point of Middle Dutch literature and continues to inspire spiritual readers.
